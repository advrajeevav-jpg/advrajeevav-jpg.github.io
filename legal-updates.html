Legal Updates

Recent changes in the law

Legislative changes and court decisions from 2023 to 2025 that affect motor accident, insurance, property, consumer, labour and family matters, with a short note on what each one means.

  • 20 Dec 2025
    Insurance

    Insurance laws amended

    The Sabka Bima Sabki Raksha (Amendment of Insurance Laws) Act, 2025 received Presidential assent on 20 December 2025. It amends the Insurance Act, 1938, the Life Insurance Corporation Act, 1956 and the IRDAI Act, 1999. Its provisions take effect on the dates the Central Government notifies.

    What it means: before relying on a provision of the Insurance Act, check whether it has been amended and whether the amendment is in force.

    Insurance guide
  • 21 Nov 2025
    Labour

    The four Labour Codes come into force

    The Code on Wages, 2019, the Industrial Relations Code, 2020, the Code on Social Security, 2020 and the Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions Code, 2020 came into force on 21 November 2025. Together they replace 29 central labour laws, including the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, the Payment of Gratuity Act, 1972, the Employees’ Compensation Act, 1923 and the Maternity Benefit Act, 1961.

    What it means: rights such as retrenchment compensation, gratuity and maternity benefit continue, now under the Codes. Fixed-term employees qualify for gratuity after one year of continuous service. Central and State rules under the Codes are being notified in stages, so check the current position for your State and industry.

    Labour & employment guide
  • 4 Nov 2025
    Motor accidents

    Supreme Court: accident claims not to be dismissed as time-barred

    Section 166(3) of the Motor Vehicles Act, 1988, in force since 1 April 2022, says a claim must be filed within six months of the accident. Its validity has been challenged before the Supreme Court. In ICICI Lombard General Insurance Co. Ltd. v. Ayiti Navaneetha, the Court directed that, while the challenge is pending, Claims Tribunals and High Courts shall not dismiss claim petitions as time-barred under that provision.

    What it means: a delayed claim should not be rejected on limitation for now. The final decision is awaited, so claims should still be filed as early as possible.

    MACT guide
  • Jul 2025
    Property

    Kerala High Court: daughters’ coparcenary rights apply in Kerala

    The Kerala High Court held that Sections 3 and 4 of the Kerala Joint Hindu Family System (Abolition) Act, 1975 are repugnant to Section 6 of the Hindu Succession Act, 1956, as amended in 2005, and cannot prevail over it. Those provisions had been relied on to deny daughters a share in ancestral property in Kerala.

    What it means: daughters of Hindu families in Kerala can claim equal shares in ancestral property, subject to the protection Section 6(5) gives to partitions completed before 20 December 2004. Check whether the ruling has been taken up in appeal.

    Property guide
  • 5 May 2025
    Motor accidents

    Cashless treatment for road accident victims

    The Cashless Treatment of Road Accident Victims Scheme, 2025, framed under Section 162 of the Motor Vehicles Act, came into force on 5 May 2025. It provides cashless treatment of up to ₹1.5 lakh per person per accident, for up to seven days from the date of the accident, at designated hospitals.

    What it means: road accident victims are eligible whether or not they have health insurance.

    MACT guide
  • 1 Jan 2025
    Consumer

    e-Jagriti replaces e-Daakhil for consumer complaints

    The Department of Consumer Affairs launched e-Jagriti, a single online platform for filing and tracking consumer complaints. It merged the earlier e-Daakhil filing portal and the CONFONET case management systems.

    What it means: consumer complaints can be filed, paid for and tracked online before the District, State and National Commissions.

    Consumer guide
  • 6 Nov 2024
    Motor accidents

    Constitution Bench on the LMV driving licence

    In Bajaj Alliance General Insurance Co. Ltd. v. Rambha Devi, a five-judge bench of the Supreme Court affirmed Mukund Dewangan (2017). A person holding a Light Motor Vehicle licence may drive a transport vehicle whose gross vehicle weight does not exceed 7,500 kg, without a separate endorsement.

    What it means: insurers cannot avoid liability merely because such a vehicle was driven by an LMV licence holder without a transport endorsement.

    MACT guide
  • 5 Sep 2024
    Insurance

    IRDAI Master Circular on protection of policyholders’ interests

    The insurance regulator consolidated its policyholder protection rules. Among other things, the free-look period for life and health policies is now 30 days. Life insurance death claims are to be settled within 15 days of intimation, or within 45 days where investigation is required.

    What it means: policyholders can measure an insurer’s delay against these timelines when making a complaint.

    Insurance guide
  • 10 Jul 2024
    Family

    Divorced Muslim women can claim maintenance under Section 125 CrPC

    In Mohd. Abdul Samad v. State of Telangana, the Supreme Court held that Section 125 of the Code of Criminal Procedure applies to all women, including divorced Muslim women. The remedy is available alongside the Muslim Women (Protection of Rights on Divorce) Act, 1986, not in place of it.

    What it means: a divorced Muslim woman may choose either remedy, or both. The equivalent provision is now Section 144 of the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023.

    Family law guide
  • 1 Jul 2024
    All areas

    New criminal laws replace the IPC, CrPC and Evidence Act

    The Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023 (BNS), the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023 (BNSS) and the Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023 (BSA) replaced the Indian Penal Code, the Code of Criminal Procedure and the Indian Evidence Act from 1 July 2024.

    What it means: familiar provisions have new numbers. For example, maintenance under Section 125 CrPC is now Section 144 BNSS, and criminal breach of trust under Section 406 IPC is now Section 316 BNS. Older provisions continue to apply to proceedings and offences that predate the change, as the transitional provisions require.

    Family law guide
  • Nov 2023
    Insurance

    Insurance Ombudsman limit raised to ₹50 lakh

    An amendment to the Insurance Ombudsman Rules, 2017 raised the value of complaints the Insurance Ombudsman can deal with from ₹30 lakh to ₹50 lakh.

    What it means: more claim disputes can now go to the Ombudsman, which charges no fee and does not require a lawyer.

    Insurance guide
